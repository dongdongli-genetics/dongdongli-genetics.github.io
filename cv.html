Motivation

My research seeks to understand how genetic variation, environmental factors, and their interactions shape complex traits and hybrid performance. I am particularly interested in dissecting genotype-by-environment interactions (G×E), quantifying the additive and non-additive genetic effects underlying heterosis, and translating these insights into more efficient and resilient breeding strategies. Ultimately, my goal is to develop statistical and genomic approaches that accelerate crop improvement, enhance resource-use efficiency, and strengthen resilience to biotic and abiotic stresses.

My research focuses on four interconnected themes:

  1. Identifying the genomic and environmental determinants of phenotypic plasticity to advance our understanding and prediction of G×E.
  2. Developing statistical genomics frameworks to characterize additive and non-additive effects, including dominance and epistatic interactions, underlying hybrid performance and heterosis, thereby informing hybrid breeding.
  3. Designing genomic selection frameworks that unlock the genetic diversity preserved in genebanks and accelerate its effective use in breeding programs.
  4. Investigating the genetic architecture of nutrient- and water-use efficiency to support the development of resource-efficient and climate-resilient crops.
